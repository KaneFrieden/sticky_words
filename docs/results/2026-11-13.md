### Dataset and selection

We use [Duolingo learning traces released by Settles and Meeder](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/N8XJME). The unit in the source file is an entry for one learner, a practice session and a lexeme.
"Lexeme" meaning the word form contains grammatical information; it is not necessarily the same thing as a lemma or an independent word.

Our initial selection keeps entries where the learning language is English (`en`) and the app interface language is Spanish (`es`). We decided on this pair because it is the largest group in the dataset and because it is a common combination for English learners. However, interface language is useful for defining a comparable group, but it does not establish a learner's native language. Users whose interface is Spanish may or may not be native Spanish speakers, and they may or may not have other language skills. The selection is therefore a practical choice for analysis, but it does not define a homogeneous population of learners.

Here we can already report some intermediary results from the first selection step. The numbers are therefore provisional and do not yet answer the research questions. The selection is also not yet filtered by time interval, and it has not excluded wildcard forms or placeholder lemmas.

The following numbers came from `subset_report.json`, produced by the script [`s01_subset_kane.py`](../../scripts/s01_subset_kane.py).

| Measure                               |     Result |

| Entries in the original dataset       | 12,854,226 |

| Distinct lexeme IDs in the selection  |      2,881 |

The selected group contains a substantial number of observations, although its size alone does not guarantee precise estimates for every word or every time interval. The 28.33% refers to dataset entries, not the proportion of learners.

## Time since previous practice

`delta` records the time since the previous practice of the item. s01 converts this value from seconds to days. No minimum time interval has been applied at this stage.

| Measure                     |                               Result |
| --------------------------- | -----------------------------------: |
| Shortest recorded interval  |               Approximately 1 second |
| Median interval             | 0.484 days, approximately 11.6 hours |
| Longest recorded interval   |                           431.1 days |
| Entries below one day       |                   2,079,220 (57.10%) |
| Entries at or above one day |                   1,561,959 (42.90%) |

Our proposal excludes intervals shorter than one day. Applying that threshold to this initial selection would remove more than half its entries, before any other exclusions. This makes the choice consequential for which practice events the final analysis represents. This means that we might have to examine the distribution and perhaps repeat the analysis with an alternative threshold. A short interval alone does not establish whether an observation occurred within the same session or during initial learning. However, since many people probably use Duolingo only once a day (to maintain their streak), a one-day threshold was a reasonable starting point for the analysis from our perspective.

## Observed answer accuracy

Across the selected entries, `session_correct` sums to **6,103,658** and `session_seen` sums to **6,752,286**. Dividing the former by the latter gives an overall observed correct-answer rate of **90.39%**.

This is a ratio of the summed counts, rather than an average across learners or across words. It includes all selected time intervals and practice histories. It is useful for describing the sample, but it does not show how accuracy changes with time or whether one word property explains another.

## Checks and next steps

Next, we will parse the lexeme information, count exclusions and construct an inventory of lemmas. Only after that will we fit the per-lemma models and assess the reliability of their estimated time slopes. The results above therefore do not yet answer H1 or H2.
